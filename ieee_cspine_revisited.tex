\begin{abstract}
Dense contextual embeddings from models such as BERT are powerful but
opaque: nearly every dimension of a 768-dimensional sentence vector is
nonzero, and no single dimension carries an assignable meaning. C-SPINE
(Contextual Sparse and Interpretable Neural Embeddings) addresses this with
a denoising sparse autoencoder that maps frozen BERT \texttt{[CLS]} vectors
to overcomplete, bounded, mostly-zero codes. This paper reports a rigorous
replication and extension. First, we audit the original manuscript against
its shipped implementation and find the architecture, denoising procedure,
and loss sign convention faithfully implemented, but also two material
discrepancies: the paper claims L2-unit input normalization that the code
never performs, and checkpoint selection peeks at the reporting split, so no
untouched test set exists. Second, we rebuild the study on a reproducible
codebase with strict train/validation/locked-test discipline, VAL-only
checkpointing, a four-level sparsity sweep, mechanism/loss ablations
including TopK, BatchTopK and dense-autoencoder controls, PCA and random
projection baselines, feature-level interpretability with purity, coherence
and neighbourhood analysis, 25-pair cross-domain transfer, and two extra
encoder families. The executed FULL Colab run (H=1024, 30 epochs, seeds
42/43/44, five domains) replicates the legacy headline almost exactly on AG
News (dense 0.903 $\rightarrow$ sparse 0.875 at 92.6\% sparsity, MSE 0.0298,
vs.\ claimed 0.903 $\rightarrow$ 0.880 at 92.76\%, MSE 0.0295) and closely on
SST-2 (0.857 $\rightarrow$ 0.815 at 90.0\% sparsity, MSE 0.0224, vs.\
claimed 0.862 $\rightarrow$ 0.811 at 89.28\%, MSE 0.0216). Generalization is
uneven: TREC holds (0.878 $\rightarrow$ 0.858), IMDB drops 6.5pp, and
11-way scientific topic classification drops 10.7pp with PCA-64 ahead by
7pp. The same run surfaces honest negatives: per-example error \emph{rises}
with recruited $L_0$ (more dims $\ne$ better fidelity); TopK reconstructs
better (VAL MSE 0.0159 vs.\ 0.0207) but wastes 88\% of its dictionary;
Hungarian-matched features across seeds agree at only 0.31 mean cosine
(H4 fails at full scale); and sparse neighbourhood overlap with dense
neighbours is partial (mean Jaccard 0.21--0.26). AG News features show
genuine category selectivity (median purity 0.84, specialization 0.89,
12/12 showcased dims majority-correct) while SST-2 sentiment is distributed
(median purity 0.60). All verdicts are evidence-linked with negatives
retained.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Modern NLP systems rely on large pretrained language models such as
BERT~\cite{devlin2019bert}. Their representations capture syntax, semantics,
and world knowledge, yet a BERT sentence vector is 768 nonzero
floating-point numbers: powerful, but a black box. In high-stakes domains
(healthcare, legal, finance) such opacity blocks explanation, so there is
practical demand for representations that stay useful while becoming
inspectable.

Sparse coding~\cite{olshausen1997sparse} is a classical remedy: force most
dimensions to zero so that each active dimension can be counted, inspected,
and potentially named. SPINE~\cite{subramanian2018spine} demonstrated this
for static word embeddings with a denoising autoencoder, CappedReLU
activations, and a two-term sparsity objective. C-SPINE ports that idea to
contextual sentence embeddings, the distribution modern systems actually
use and a harder one: context-dependent, high-variance, and richer in
covariance structure than static word-vector tables.

This paper is a replication and extension, not a new method. Our remit is
the original C-SPINE contribution itself: verify what was claimed, fix what
was methodologically weak, and test generalizability. A companion effort
covers knowledge-graph extensions and is out of scope here. Our objective is
scientific rigor and reproducibility rather than maximal scores. Concretely
we contribute: (i) a paper-vs-code audit with two material discrepancies
documented; (ii) a clean, configuration-driven codebase (\texttt{src/})
with strict train/validation/locked-test discipline; (iii) a controlled
sparsity sweep, mechanism/loss ablations, and information-preservation
baselines; (iv) feature-level interpretability (purity curves, firing
statistics, dense-space coherence, co-activation, neighbour preservation)
and 25-pair cross-domain transfer; (v) executed FULL-scale results over five
domains with honest negatives retained; and (vi) hypothesis verdicts H1--H6
against pre-registered evidence.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Static vs.\ Contextual Embeddings}
Word2Vec~\cite{mikolov2013distributed} and GloVe~\cite{pennington2014glove}
assign one vector per word type and cannot distinguish senses. Contextual
models (BERT~\cite{devlin2019bert}, RoBERTa~\cite{liu2019roberta},
GPT-2~\cite{radford2019language}) condition on context via self-attention
and masked-language pretraining. The final-layer \texttt{[CLS]} hidden state
serves as a sentence representation and is the input to C-SPINE.

\subsection{Sparse Coding and Dictionary Learning}
Olshausen and Field~\cite{olshausen1997sparse} established overcomplete
sparse bases for efficient coding. Dictionary learning, ICA, and NMF extend
the theme of finding interpretable components. Overcompleteness
($H>D$; here $1024>768$) is deliberate: extra atoms let the
signal spread thinly so that each input uses a different sparse subset.

\subsection{SPINE}
Subramanian et al.~\cite{subramanian2018spine} trained a denoising sparse
autoencoder on static embeddings with CappedReLU units bounded to
$[0,1]$, reporting roughly 90\% sparsity with small downstream drops and
topic-selective dimensions. C-SPINE reuses the architecture and the
average-sparsity vs.\ peak-sparsity tension, applied to sentence-level
contextual vectors.

\subsection{Sparse Autoencoders for Interpretability}
SAEs are now a standard mechanistic-interpretability tool for decomposing
model internals into human-meaningful features~\cite{bricken2023towards}.
C-SPINE differs in target: output sentence vectors for downstream reuse
rather than internal circuits. Modern hard-sparsity variants such as TopK
and BatchTopK~\cite{gao2024scaling} replace soft penalties with exact
budgets; we include both as ablations to test whether C-SPINE behavior is
objective-specific or generic sparse coding.

\subsection{Probing Classifiers}
Linear probes test whether class information is linearly accessible in
frozen features~\cite{tenney2019bert}. We use logistic-regression probes
throughout under one fixed protocol across dense, reconstructed, sparse,
and baseline arms.

\section{Audit: Manuscript vs.\ Implementation}
Treating the paper and the shipped code as independent sources of truth, we
verified seventeen pipeline points (representations, pooling,
normalization, encoder/decoder, activation, both sparsity terms,
reconstruction objective, noise, training, splits, checkpointing, probe,
metrics, seeds, hardware, every reported number). Three findings matter.

First, the architecture, denoising placement (noise at train time only,
reconstruction against the clean target), and the ASL/PSL sign convention
($\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{MSE}+\lambda_1\mathrm{ASL}-\lambda_2\mathrm{PSL}$)
match exactly, as do the optimizer settings and the CLS-pooling pipeline.

Second, two material discrepancies. (a)~The manuscript states twice that
vectors are L2-normalized to unit length; the code never normalizes
anywhere. The FULL run confirms raw CLS norms of $14.43\pm0.44$, so the
$\sigma=0.1$ noise is only $\sim$0.7\% relative magnitude (weak denoising
pressure as configured). (b)~Checkpoint selection uses best validation MSE,
but the ``validation'' split \emph{is} the reporting split (AG News test;
SST-2 validation reused for reporting), so no untouched test set exists and
reported accuracies are optimistic. All headline numbers are additionally
single-seed point estimates without uncertainty, the reconstruction loss is
a batch-mean MSE rather than the stated sum (rescaling the effective
$\lambda$ weights), and several figures are placeholders.

Third, the original manuscript's Table~II values (SST-2 86.24\% dense
$\rightarrow$ 81.08\% sparse at 89.28\% sparsity; AG News 90.30\%
$\rightarrow$ 87.96\% at 92.76\%) were arithmetically self-consistent but
unverifiable before; \S\ref{sec:results} now tests them directly against
locked-TEST reruns.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Feature Extraction}
A frozen encoder maps tokenized input (max 128 subword tokens) to a
sentence vector: \texttt{[CLS]} final hidden state for BERT-family models,
masked-mean pooling for MiniLM-family models. An explicit
\texttt{normalize\_inputs} flag reproduces legacy behavior
(\texttt{False}) or tests the paper's claim (\texttt{True}).
BERT is never fine-tuned; embeddings are cached once.

\subsection{Formal Problem Definition}
Let $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^D$ denote a frozen sentence embedding and let
$H$ be the dictionary width. The central goal is to learn an overcomplete,
sparse code $\mathbf{z}\in[0,1]^H$ and a decoder that approximately
preserves $\mathbf{x}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^D \xrightarrow{\;f_\theta\;} \mathbf{z}\in[0,1]^H
\xrightarrow{\;g_\phi\;} \hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^D, \qquad H>D.
\label{eq:pipeline}
\end{equation}
The model is intentionally overcomplete. For the main experiments, $D=768$
and $H=1024$, so there are more latent atoms than input coordinates.
Overcompleteness does not by itself produce sparsity; the loss must make
inactive coordinates preferable while preserving enough active coordinates
to reconstruct the input.

At training time only, a denoising perturbation is added:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{x} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \qquad
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^2\mathbf{I}), \qquad
\sigma=0.1.
\label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
The target remains the clean vector $\mathbf{x}$. Thus the model is not
trained to reproduce noise; it is trained to recover the uncorrupted
representation from a perturbed observation.

\subsection{Model}
The legacy C-SPINE encoder is $\mathbf{z}=\mathrm{CappedReLU}
(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{enc}}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{enc}})$
with $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:noise}, at train time only,
following the denoising framework~\cite{vincent2008extracting}; the decoder
$\hat{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{dec}}\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{dec}}$
is linear. Variants: TopKSAE (exact per-example $L_0=k$), BatchTopKSAE
(exact batch budget $k\cdot B$), DenseAE (ReLU, MSE-only no-sparsity
control), and a two-layer MLP encoder (capacity control). A factory guard
warns loudly on undercomplete $H<D$ configurations after we observed one
densify instead of sparsify.

\subsection{Loss}
The implemented objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathrm{MSE}(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{x})
+ \lambda_{1}\,\mathrm{ASL}(\mathbf{z}) - \lambda_{2}\,\mathrm{PSL}(\mathbf{z})
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where the batch contains $B$ examples and the dictionary has $H$ features.
The reconstruction term is the elementwise batch mean,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MSE} = \frac{1}{BD}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{d=1}^{D}
(\hat{x}_{bd}-x_{bd})^2.
\label{eq:mse}
\end{equation}
The average sparsity loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ASL} = \frac{1}{BH}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{j=1}^{H} z_{bj}.
\label{eq:asl}
\end{equation}
Because $z_{bj}\ge 0$, minimizing ASL pushes the whole code toward zero.
This is the term that pays for sparsity: removing it sharply improves
reconstruction because the model can activate substantially more units.

The peak sparsity loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PSL} = \frac{1}{H}\sum_{j=1}^{H}\max_{1\le b\le B} z_{bj}.
\label{eq:psl}
\end{equation}
PSL is subtracted from the objective. Consequently, the optimizer is
rewarded when every feature reaches a substantial activation on at least
some training examples. This counters the dead-feature failure mode that
would arise from minimizing ASL alone. Because CappedReLU bounds
$z_j\in[0,1]$, PSL cannot be made arbitrarily large.

With the default $\lambda_1=\lambda_2=1$, the three forces can be read as
follows: MSE asks the dictionary to preserve information, ASL asks it to
use as few features as possible, and PSL asks those features to be
collectively utilized rather than concentrating all activity into a small
subset of atoms.

The code implements an additional L1 ablation,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{L1}(\mathbf{z}) = \frac{1}{BH}\sum_{b,j} |z_{bj}|,
\label{eq:l1}
\end{equation}
which replaces the ASL+PSL pair. Since CappedReLU is nonnegative, L1 is
numerically close to the same first-order activation pressure as ASL; what
it removes is the explicit anti-dead-feature reward represented by PSL.

$\lambda_1=\lambda_2=1.0$ by default, optimized with
Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam}; ablations toggle each term
(no-ASL/no-PSL/no-noise/MSE-only/L1-instead) to attribute effects.

\subsection{Metrics and What Each Formula Measures}
For an individual sparse code $\mathbf{z}_i$, the recruited feature count
is
\begin{equation}
L_0(\mathbf{z}_i) = \sum_{j=1}^{H} \mathbb{1}[z_{ij}\ne 0],
\label{eq:l0}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding zero fraction is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Sparsity}(\mathbf{z}_i) = 1 - \frac{L_0(\mathbf{z}_i)}{H}.
\label{eq:sparsity}
\end{equation}
The reported mean $L_0$ is therefore the average number of active
dictionary atoms per sentence, while the reported sparsity ratio is the
average fraction of dictionary entries that are exactly zero. A feature is
called dead if its activation is zero for every evaluation example:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{dead}(j) = \mathbb{1}\Big[\textstyle\sum_i \mathbb{1}[z_{ij}\ne 0] = 0\Big].
\label{eq:dead}
\end{equation}
Feature utilization at a threshold $\tau$ is the fraction of features whose
activation frequency is at least $\tau$.

Reconstruction quality is measured by cosine similarity,
\begin{equation}
\cos(\mathbf{x},\hat{\mathbf{x}}) =
\frac{\mathbf{x}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{x}}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|_2\,\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_2},
\label{eq:cosine}
\end{equation}
explained variance,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EV} = 1 - \frac{\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf{x}-\hat{\mathbf{x}})}{\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf{x})},
\label{eq:ev}
\end{equation}
and normalized reconstruction error,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NMSE} = \frac{\mathrm{MSE}(\mathbf{x},\hat{\mathbf{x}})}
{\mathrm{mean}_i\|\mathbf{x}_i\|_2^2 / D}.
\label{eq:nmse}
\end{equation}
The code additionally keeps the full per-example MSE distribution (mean,
standard deviation, median, 90th percentile, and 99th percentile), because
a single mean can conceal a tail of difficult examples.

\subsection{Downstream Probing as an Information-Preservation Test}
Let $\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{te}}$ denote one
representation arm, with labels $y$. The downstream test fits the same
logistic-regression protocol to every representation:
\begin{equation}
p(y=c\mid\mathbf{r}) = \mathrm{softmax}_c(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{b}),
\label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}
using $C=1.0$, the LBFGS solver, and at most 1000 iterations. Dense-like
representations are standardized using training-set statistics; sparse
$[0,1]$ codes are not standardized. The reported primary metrics are
accuracy and macro-F1, with micro-F1, macro precision/recall, and per-class
F1 retained in the metric files.

The critical comparison is not only sparse versus dense. Reconstructed
dense vectors isolate information lost by the autoencoder mapping from
information lost specifically because the downstream classifier consumes
sparse codes. PCA-64 and Gaussian random projection-64 then ask whether
comparable performance is obtainable by ordinary dimensionality reduction.

\subsection{Hypotheses}
H1: sparse codes retain most downstream information at far fewer active
dims. H2: a sparsity plateau exists where fidelity degrades slowly.
H3: the C-SPINE objective yields more coherent features than a non-sparse
autoencoder. H4: features recur across seeds. H5: the method stays useful
across domains. H6: the effect exceeds ordinary dimensionality reduction.
Each maps to pre-registered evidence (sweeps, ablations, matching,
transfer, PCA/RP baselines).

\subsection{Sparsity Sweep (H2)}
On SST-2, $\lambda_{\mathrm{ASL}}\in\{0.1,1,3,8\}$ yields $L_0$
212.9/102.7/70.6/34.6 (79.2\%/90.0\%/93.1\%/96.6\% zeros) with MSE
0.0185/0.0224/0.0234/0.0253 and sparse accuracy 0.846/0.815/0.797/0.773
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}; Table~\ref{tab:sweep}). Unlike the smoke run there
is no flat plateau followed by a cliff: accuracy falls 7.3pp monotonically
while MSE rises 37\%, and dead features climb 0.7\%/4.1\%/16.5\%/31.7\%.

Over 30 epochs MSE falls and sparsity rises smoothly on every domain with

\subsection{Reconstruction Quality vs.\ Recruited Dims}
The binned recon-vs-$L_0$ curves invert the smoke-run reading and do so on
four of five datasets (Fig.~\ref{fig:reconl0}). On SST-2, per-example MSE
\emph{rises} with $L_0$: 0.0182 at $L_0\!\sim\!$9--41 climbing to 0.0279 at
$L_0\!\sim\!$163--367. AG News shows the same slope (0.0275 $\rightarrow$
0.0350); IMDB is flat through $L_0\!\sim\!$115 then jumps to 0.0344 in the
top bin; arXiv drifts 0.0247 $\rightarrow$ 0.0302; only TREC is U-shaped
(best 0.0094 mid-range, worst 0.0161 at the top). More active dims do
\emph{not} buy better fidelity at full scale. The natural interpretation is
that $L_0$ indexes example difficulty rather than a fidelity dial: harder
inputs recruit more atoms yet still reconstruct worse. This reframes H2 ---
sparsity is a property of the input under the learned dictionary, not a
knob that trades fidelity monotonically.

\section{Ablations}
\subsection{Sparsity Mechanism}
At matched $H=1024$ on SST-2, TopK ($k=64$) reaches lower VAL MSE than
C-SPINE (0.0159 vs.\ 0.0207) at fixed $L_0=64$, but wastes 904/1024
dictionary atoms (88.3\% dead; BatchTopK nearly identical at 88.6\% dead,

The dense AE reconstructs best (0.0030) with 59.4\% dead ReLUs and merely
incidental 60.1\% ``sparsity'' at $L_0=408$. Fidelity and utilization point
in opposite directions: neither arm wins outright, and the soft ASL/PSL
objective's distinctive contribution is dictionary utilization, not raw
MSE (Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}).

\subsection{Loss Ablations}
Removing terms one at a time (SST-2 VAL MSE): full 0.02069; no-PSL 0.02027;
no-noise 0.02044 --- all within noise of each other, so neither the PSL
reward nor the $\sigma=0.1$ denoising pressure buys reconstruction at this
scale. Two arms move decisively: no-ASL drops to 0.01205 and MSE-only to
0.00446. The reading is unambiguous and theoretically expected --- the
average-sparsity penalty is what the MSE pays for; delete it and fidelity
recovers because the code is allowed to densify. The ablation therefore
confirms ASL is the binding sparsity constraint while PSL and noise are
second-order for reconstruction (their role, if any, is in dictionary
health, not fidelity).

\subsection{Representation Notes}
Raw CLS norms average $14.43\pm0.44$, confirming the audit: the legacy setup
never produced unit-length inputs, and $\sigma=0.1$ noise is $\sim$0.7\%
relative magnitude (weak denoising pressure as configured). The
\texttt{normalize\_inputs} ablation remains queued; nothing in the FULL run
rehabilitates the paper's normalization claim.

\section{Generalization and Transfer}
Frozen-SAE transfer over all 25 ordered domain pairs (no retraining) shows
partial reuse with clear dataset tuning (Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}).
In-domain diagonals (MSE) are 0.0224/0.0298/0.0250/0.0112/0.0267 for
SST-2/AG/IMDB/TREC/arXiv with cosines 0.959/0.946/0.954/0.981/0.956.
Off-diagonal MSE is 1.5--4$\times$ worse in most directions (e.g.\
SST-2$\rightarrow$arXiv 0.0918, TREC$\rightarrow$arXiv 0.1091,
arXiv$\rightarrow$SST-2 0.0794) while cosine degrades gracefully
(0.826--0.955), so directions partially transfer but scales do not. Two
asymmetries stand out. First, the TREC dictionary over-fires abroad ($L_0$
233 on SST-2, 465 on AG News, 521 on arXiv vs.\ 76 at home) while visiting
dictionaries collapse on TREC (SST-2$\rightarrow$TREC $L_0$ 33.8 with
69.6\% dead; arXiv$\rightarrow$TREC 57.8\% dead; AG$\rightarrow$SST-2
41.9\% dead). Short questions appear to induce a compact, trigger-happy
dictionary that cannot tile other manifolds, and other dictionaries leave
most atoms silent on questions. Second, same-family sentiment transfer is
the healthiest pair (SST-2$\leftrightarrow$IMDB cosines 0.917/0.943, MSE
0.0451/0.0326). Encoder probes are preliminary (3 epochs each):
MiniLM ($D=384$) reaches VAL MSE 0.0463 while DistilBERT ($D=768$) reaches
0.0254 but its sparsity \emph{fell} 24.5\%$\rightarrow$16.3\% over the
window --- densifying, not sparsifying, early in training. No
encoder-agnostic claim is supported.

\section{Feature Stability}
\label{sec:stability}
With three seeds available, H4 is now testable on SST-2 --- and it fails.
Hungarian matching on decoder directions (seeds 42/43) gives mean matched
cosine 0.305 (median 0.282; only 0.6\% of pairs above 0.7 over 1024
matches). Matched activation correlation averages 0.385 (40.3\% above 0.5),
and top-20 activating-example overlap averages Jaccard 0.063 (median
0.026). Dictionaries with equivalent loss values (VAL MSE within 0.0003)
learn nearly disjoint atoms: optimization is seed-stable, identity is not.
An interpretable representation should partially survive
re-initialization; C-SPINE's does not at this scale. Single-run heatmaps,
however clean, must therefore not be read as stable concepts. The
smoke-run deferral is resolved against H4.

Each run is parameterized by a serializable configuration containing at

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
Downstream figures are seed-42 single runs (training VAL MSE is
triple-seed; TEST was touched once per the locked protocol, so multi-seed
TEST means are unavailable by design). TREC deltas ($\pm$4pp at $n=500$)
are noise-compatible. The probe scaling asymmetry (StandardScaler on
dense-like arms, none on sparse codes) can shift small deltas and must be
sensitivity-tested before any sparse-vs-dense ranking claim; AG News
sparse-vs-PCA (0.875 vs.\ 0.874) is a tie under any such shift. Sparsity
counts exact zeros (valid for CappedReLU; $\epsilon$-thresholded for ReLU
variants) and DenseAE ``sparsity'' is incidental. English classification
only; NLI, coreference, and QA may be harder for sparse codes. Purity is
correlation evidence, not human interpretability; no human ratings were
collected. Saturation ties at activation 1.000 weaken global-top mismatch
checks. The 95\% CI normal approximation at $n=3$ would be wide; we report
ranges instead.

\section{Reproducibility}
Every run is reproducible from a JSON config recording seed, dataset,
encoder, pooling, dims, hyperparameters, sparsity settings, checkpoint, and
metrics; VAL-only checkpointing and single-touch locked TEST are enforced
in code; all skips/failures are recorded explicitly rather than silently
(\texttt{metrics/status.json} shows 100\% PASS, zero skips across data,
embedding, training, eval, sweep, ablations, transfer, encoders, and
qualitative stages). Optimization determinism holds (seed VAL-MSE spreads
$\le$0.0003). The archive \texttt{final\_research (2).zip} contains the
codebase, notebook, configs, results, metrics, plots, tables
(\texttt{qual\_*} paper-ready txt/md/tex/csv), checkpoint metadata, logs,
and reports.

\section{Conclusion}
C-SPINE is a well-posed port of SPINE-style denoising sparse coding to
contextual sentence vectors whose implementation matches its equations but
not all its prose (no L2 normalization as claimed) nor its evaluation
hygiene (leaked checkpoint selection, single seed, thin metrics). At full
scale ($H=1024$, 30 epochs, five domains), the legacy AG News headline
replicates almost exactly (0.903 $\rightarrow$ 0.875 at 92.6\% sparsity,
MSE 0.0298) and SST-2 closely (0.857 $\rightarrow$ 0.815 at 90.0\%, MSE
0.0224); TREC holds while IMDB ($-$6.5pp) and 11-way scientific topics (arXiv-11)
($-$10.7pp, PCA +7pp) do not. Feature quality is real but narrow: AG News
atoms are category-selective with flat purity curves and coherent
neighbourhoods, SST-2 sentiment is distributed, and dictionaries do not
survive re-initialization (matched cosine 0.31). Counterweights are
explicit: PCA leads or ties everywhere except one tie, TopK reconstructs
better but wastes 88\% of its dictionary, ASL is the binding fidelity cost,
per-example error rises with $L_0$, and neighbourhood preservation is
partial (Jaccard $\sim$0.2). Hypothesis verdicts: H1 supported on
news/questions/sentiment, fails on scientific topics; H2 weakly supported
(monotone 7.3pp cost, no plateau); H3 unsupported as stated (no coherence
advantage over dense-AE demonstrated; utilization is the real distinction);
H4 fails; H5 mixed (sentiment/topic yes, scientific no); H6 unconfirmed
(one tie, four losses). The apparatus --- modular codebase, locked-test
discipline, full metric suites, sweeps, ablations, baselines, stability and
transfer protocols, pre-registered H1--H6, and a self-contained Colab
notebook packaging everything into one archive --- is delivered and
full-scale validated, with negative results retained.

\section{Additional Mathematical and Experimental Details}
\subsection{Why ASL and PSL Pull in Opposite Directions}
Consider a single feature $j$. ASL contributes approximately a positive
first derivative with respect to every activation, encouraging
$z_{bj}\rightarrow 0$ for all examples. PSL contributes a negative
derivative only through the batch maximum, encouraging at least one
example in the batch to maintain a large activation. The combination
therefore behaves like a soft ``usage'' objective: features are expensive
to use on average, but a feature that never fires is not rewarded. This is
qualitatively different from an unconstrained ReLU autoencoder, whose
hidden units can remain broadly active whenever that improves MSE.

\subsection{Hard-Sparsity Ablations}
For TopK, the encoder first computes a ReLU activation $h$ and then keeps
the $k$ largest entries per example:
\begin{equation}
z_j = h_j\,\mathbb{1}[h_j \ge \tau_i], \qquad \tau_i = \text{$k$-th largest } h_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Thus each example has an exact $L_0=k$ before ties, and the subsequent cap
places surviving activations in $[0,1]$. BatchTopK instead keeps the
largest $k\cdot B$ values over the whole batch, so the sum of the
per-example $L_0$ values is fixed while individual examples may receive
different numbers of atoms. The experiment is useful because it separates
two questions: whether exact sparsity itself is enough, and whether
C-SPINE's soft lifetime objective is needed to distribute usage across a
large dictionary.

\subsection{Dimensionality-Reduction Baselines}
PCA solves the rank-$k$ least-squares projection problem on the training
embeddings and evaluates the resulting coordinates on test embeddings. In
matrix form,
\begin{equation}
Z = X U_k,
\end{equation}
where $U_k$ contains the first $k$ principal directions. The
random-projection baseline instead draws a fixed Gaussian projection matrix
$R$ and uses $Z=XR$. These baselines have no learned sparse semantics; they
provide an information-preservation control at a similar final coordinate
budget.

\subsection{Transfer Protocol}
For every ordered pair $(A,B)$ of the five datasets, a C-SPINE model is
trained only on $A$, frozen, and evaluated on $B$ without retraining or
refitting the dictionary. There are $5\times5=25$ directions including the
five in-domain diagonals. This makes a useful distinction between
within-domain compression and reusable dictionary geometry. The strongest
transfer found is within the sentiment pair SST-2$\leftrightarrow$IMDB,
while the most obvious failure mode is the TREC dictionary, which
over-fires badly on other corpora.

\subsection{Audit of the Legacy Claims}
The audit checked representations, pooling, normalization, activation,
noise placement, loss sign, optimization, splitting, checkpoint selection,
probe design, metrics, seeds and reported arithmetic. The implementation
faithfully matches the architecture and denoising loss, but two claims in
the legacy manuscript are material mismatches: input vectors were not
actually L2-normalized, and the reporting split was reused for model
selection and final accuracy. The new experiment therefore treats the
paper's numerical claims as historical reference values rather than as a
clean test set. The corrected pipeline restores a true validation split
and an untouched reporting split.
